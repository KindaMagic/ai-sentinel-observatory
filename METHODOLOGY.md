# AI Sentinel Observatory · Public methodology v0.7
**Prepared:** 27 October 2026. **Status:** Independent public-interest pilot; human safety review pending.

## Purpose
Provide a transparent, readable evidence register of publicly documented events related to AI control, containment, security and oversight. This is an early-warning **research aid**, not real-time operational monitoring, an extinction-risk forecast, a safety certification or an emergency-response service.

## Boundary between components
- **Public automatic watch**: GitHub Actions runs `scripts/monitor.py` every six hours (four checks a day), reading selected public publication feeds, applying conservative transparent keyword screening, and publishing `watch.json` to the public repository. It requires no OpenAI API key or paid model inference. These are **preliminary source signals, not verified incidents**.
- **Public light**: the site reads `watch.json` when loaded. Green = no recent keyword flags in the available monitored subset (not an all-clear); Amber = one or more relevant publication signals requiring examination. **Orange and Red are never automatically assigned** and require human evidence review. Unavailable or stale data are labelled as such. The selected sources do not cover the whole world.
- **Editorial incidents**: `incidents.json` contains three source-linked cases maintained through human editorial updates, separate from the automatically generated watch. The formal public severity remains unassigned until human validation.
- **Cumulative critical incidents**: `critical-incidents.json` is a distinct, inspectable archive reserved for documented serious real-world harm, major disruption or consequential AI-control failure. No existing contextual case is automatically classified as critical; the automated watch cannot add or modify this archive. Entries require primary documentation, a stable identifier, explicit confirmed consequences, uncertainties and dated corrections. See `CRITICAL_REGISTER.md`. An empty archive is not an all-clear.
- **Private ChatGPT watch**: a separate private scheduled research task checks broader public sources and may notify the owner. It does not write to the GitHub repository.
- **Offline register v0.1**: browser-local records are not automatically shared, published or backed up.

## Sources and observations
1. Record the original event date separately from the date a report was published.
2. Provide direct URLs to primary sources and note their institutional roles (operator, independent evaluator, regulator, investigative reporter).
3. Distinguish test environment from real deployment and confirmed consequences from attempted actions.
4. Give the most important alternative explanation, uncertainty or missing verification.
5. Do not assume a company's statement about remediation is independently verified.
6. Distinguish a new incident from a follow-up report about an old incident.

## Human-approved triage rubric
- **Green**: no qualifying new event found in the observed sources; not an all-clear.
- **Amber**: credible risk signal or emerging but unresolved event.
- **Orange**: significant primary-documented real-world control failure or independently documented major oversight weakness; requires escalation for specialist review.
- **Red**: severe ongoing real-world loss-of-control incident independently corroborated and expressly confirmed by a qualified human reviewer.

**No human-validated overall danger rating is assigned in the pilot.** An earlier version automatically displayed Orange because two feeds reported on the same UK AISI incident. That was not a reliable basis for escalation; on 27 October the automatic warning was limited to Green/Amber screening only. Human review must precede any Orange/Red public alert.

## Automatically refreshed provisional risk indicator
The website uses **four lights (Green, Amber, Orange, Red)**. Green/Amber only are selected by a deterministic, open-source keyword screener over articles published within the prior 14 days from four monitored public feeds (OpenAI, UK AISI via Alan Turing Institute, Anthropic research via Alan Turing Institute and Hugging Face). **Orange and Red cannot be generated by this automation.** Different sources can cover the very same event; multiple reports are not independent incidents. Routine positive security announcements should not trigger scrutiny solely for mentioning risks, vulnerabilities or red-teaming.

The public light includes a last-checked UTC time, feed coverage, and supporting publication links. If fewer than two feeds respond, the display must show **unavailable**. If a record is older than 36 hours, it is marked **stale**. This process monitors publication *signals*, not the operational state of AI, and must never be described as a live global safety score.

The separately maintained `incidents.json` captures detailed historical evidence. Human editorial approval, documented review and independent verification remain prerequisites for formal high-severity conclusions and any reporting to authorities.

## Dated corrections and changes
- **27 October 2026 (v0.7)**: Withdrew automatically assigned Orange after detecting two publications about one underlying AISI event. Constrained the automatic screening outcome to Green/Amber; removed generic positive security-article keywords; clarified that stale results have no lit indicator. The event remains in the source list for careful human review, not a declared global AI warning.

## Routing evidence to people, not automatic emergency calls
Public contact information is provided to help visitors choose an official reporting route when they have genuine evidence of an incident. Emergency numbers are reserved for immediate threats, not a colour on this site. NCSC, the police, Report Fraud and ICO have different remits and reporting requirements. Security incidents, crimes and personal-data breaches must be routed according to the underlying real-world facts. The website never phones, emails or submits allegations automatically, and the AI Security Institute is not represented as a public emergency hotline. Contact sources were checked on 26 October 2026.

## Publishing and governance guardrails
- No automatically published Orange/Red alerts; human editor authorises each public revision.
- At least two sources for major claims wherever available; where unavailable, publish the verification gap plainly.
- Red should require an accountable qualified human reviewer plus second independent expert review, if time permits.
- Never browse purported secrets, seek restricted access, probe third-party systems or run potentially dangerous demonstrations.
- No automated control, shutdown, external messaging, accusations or enforcement.
- Keep a dated public corrections log for amendments; preserve removed claims with explanations.
- No identifying personal data of developers, targets or security researchers unless necessary and already responsibly public; do not reproduce exploit steps.

## Publication checklist
[ ] Human editor reviews every entry and original source.
[ ] Confirm all URLs are available and editorial summaries accurately reflect those sources.
[ ] Confirm the date on the page, incident ID references and public review status.
[ ] Establish an accountable editorial contact and a corrections channel before soliciting public submissions.
[ ] Apply a version tag, save an immutable local copy of the published JSON and HTML.
[ ] Use static hosting with HTTPS; do not add analytics or forms without considering privacy obligations.
[ ] Follow up serious claims with independent expert validation before making extraordinary warnings.

## Limitations
This prototype provides no live model telemetry; an unreported incident will be invisible. It cannot enforce guardrails on external AI developers or prevent catastrophic outcomes. A model assessing AI safety is not impartial self-verification.
